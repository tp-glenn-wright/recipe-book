otherwise. Good enough for a recipe book, not good enough for a clinical diet. Where a
figure is zero or trivial it is left blank.

## Cooking loss factors

Applied to the total raw mass to get the finished mass. This is the step most often
skipped, and skipping it inflates per-100g figures.

| Cooking method | Finished mass |
|---|---|
| Braise or stew, lid off, long | 70% of raw |
| Braise or stew, lid on | 80% of raw |
| Roast meat | 75% of raw |
| Pan fried meat | 75% of raw |
| Grilled or barbecued meat | 75% of raw |
| Baked goods (biscuits, cakes) | 85% of raw |
| Bread | 88% of raw |
| Soup, lid on | 90% of raw |
| Reduced sauce | judge it, often 50% or less |
| No cooking (salad, dressing) | 100% of raw |

Ingredients that absorb rather than lose, so add water to the finished mass:

| Ingredient | Cooked mass |
|---|---|
| Rice | 3× dry weight |
| Pasta | 2.5× dry weight |
| Dried lentils or beans | 2.5× dry weight |
| Couscous | 3× dry weight |

## Things that are not eaten

Subtract these before working out the totals, or the numbers come out badly wrong:

- Poaching, blanching and boiling water that gets drained.
- A marinade that is discarded rather than cooked down. If half the marinade goes in the
  bin, count half.
- Frying oil that stays in the pan. For shallow frying, assume the food picks up about a
  fifth of the oil; for deep frying, about 10% of the food's weight in oil.
- Bones, shells and aromatics that get fished out, like a cinnamon quill, lime leaves or
  a bouquet garni. They contribute flavour and almost no mass.
